A perspective

When parenting advice feels like too much

The Baby authors suggest choosing a few useful sources when parenting information becomes overwhelming. Both books pair learning with observing your own child and seeking support for questions about their development.

In the everyday

When another book, course, or opinion leaves you with more to keep up with.

Learning can begin with a question about the child in front of you. The Baby authors include observation alongside reading and other resources, and explicitly say that it is possible to limit how much information you take in.

Davies encourages learning about a child’s differences and getting the support they need. Courses, podcasts, and books are possibilities in her account; her adult-preparation chapter offers practices that may fit some families and not others.

Neither list has to become your reading assignment. The books also make room for learning about things you enjoy beyond parenting.
