An everyday situation

When your child turns down help

During an activity, an offer of help can be refused. Davies makes room for the frustration and anger that may follow, and for another chance to try later.

In the everyday

Your child is struggling with an activity but pushes away your hand or says they do not want help.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Davies gives no fixed age for this situation within the toddler years.

Relevant when your child is exploring an activity, struggling, and declining offered help.

The amount of help and the form of the child’s refusal may differ; this is not a test of independence.

Davies suggests offering a small demonstration or a short cue when a child is about to give up. If they welcome it, the adult helps with that part and then sees how the child continues.

Sometimes the child refuses every offer and becomes angry. In the book’s account, that feeling is allowed too; the activity can wait for another attempt. The child need not accept a demonstration or finish the task now.

This discussion concerns an activity the child is exploring. Supervision still applies to small parts and sharp objects, and the adult’s responsibility for necessary care or immediate protection remains.
