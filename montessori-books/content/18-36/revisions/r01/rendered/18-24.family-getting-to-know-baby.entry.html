A perspective

Welcoming a baby or adopted child

The Baby authors describe time to get to know each other after birth or adoption. They include support for the adults, room for feelings of love that take time, and a doctor’s help when needed for postpartum depression.

In the everyday

When a newborn arrives, or an adopted child joins your family.

Age, interest & context

Here this is about a child joining the family through adoption, not a new stage expected at this age.

The authors call the first six to eight weeks after birth “symbiosis,” or life together. Ordinary care, talking, singing, and noticing a baby’s rhythms are ways of becoming familiar with one another.

They suggest simplifying social commitments where possible and accepting practical help. They also recognize that a quiet home may be difficult with older siblings, and that help depends on the people and resources available to each family.

For adoption, they suggest a similar period of getting acquainted whenever the child arrives. Familiar voices, gentle care, and predictable places and rhythms are their focus. Their six-to-eight-week suggestion describes time together; it is not an attachment deadline.

The authors explicitly include caregivers who did not carry the baby, parents who return to work soon after birth, and families who do not breastfeed. They acknowledge that love may not feel immediate and direct parents toward a doctor when needed for postpartum depression, alongside accepting support from others.
