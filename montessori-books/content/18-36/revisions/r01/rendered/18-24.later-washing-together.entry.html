A perspective

A turn in washing face and body

Davies includes cleaning the face and washing the body among the care activities an older toddler can join. Her chart describes growing participation with help, within care shared with an adult.

In the everyday

Your child wants to join a movement you are making during washing.

Age, interest & context

In the book: The practical-life chart lists washing the body and cleaning the face for 18 months to 3 years, continuing earlier skills with help.

Interest in joining care; the chart gives a broad age indication, not a requirement to complete washing independently.

A shared-care perspective; the chart does not set a bathing procedure or a self-care deadline.

The 18-month-to-3-year chart names face cleaning and body washing. For the body-washing example, Davies mentions a small travel-size soap bottle to reduce waste; she does not supply a bathing procedure in that list.

Her wider care discussion treats washing as time for communication and respectful touch. Talk about what is happening and listen to the child’s response, including gestures or sounds.

The child’s turn can be only part of the care. Davies keeps gentle adult assistance and a brief explanation alongside necessary care; participation does not mean managing the whole routine alone.
