A perspective

When preschool is coming up

For families preparing for preschool or school, Davies describes familiar everyday participation, getting to know another caregiver, and adult help with social moments. These can be part of preparing for the change.

In the everyday

When your family has an upcoming preschool or school start.

Age, interest & context

For an actual upcoming start; the book gives no starting age here.

Her everyday examples include a jacket, shoes, and a tissue. An adult can support the part the child is trying; these examples do not need to become a list of skills to pass before school.

If another caregiver is new, Davies suggests first spending time together reading or playing. Once the child is comfortable with that person, she describes short separations that can gradually become longer. She emphasizes telling the child when you leave, even when goodbye brings sadness. In her account of working with other caregivers, she also recalls using a brief, warm goodbye and offering a hug at reunion if her child wanted one.

At the playground, she describes translating a child’s meaning, helping them stand up for themselves when needed, and modeling care for other people. Preparing can include this adult support.
