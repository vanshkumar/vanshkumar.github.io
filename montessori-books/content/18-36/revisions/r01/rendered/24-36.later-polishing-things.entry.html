A perspective

The rubbing movement in polishing

Davies includes polishing among supervised household activities. Her mirror and wood examples specify products she calls nontoxic, applied with a small sponge or finger mitt.

In the everyday

Your child is interested in rubbing a surface with a cloth or mitt.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Mirror and wood polishing: 18 months–2+ years. Shoe polishing: 2.5 years.

Interest in rubbing or polishing, with supervision; the later shoe example is kept separate.

The mirror setup includes a small container, an applicator sponge, a finger mitt, and an underlay. The wood example uses a manageable container, a small dish, a finger mitt, and polish such as beeswax.

She describes shoe polishing separately, at 2½ years, with a little polish, a finger mitt, a soft brush, and an underlay covering the table. This is a later example rather than another material to add to the earlier setup.

These lists describe Davies’s activities; they do not identify a particular product for your home. A simple cloth for dusting is another of her household examples when that is the work you want to share.
