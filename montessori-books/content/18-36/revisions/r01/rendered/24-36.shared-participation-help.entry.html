A perspective

Joining in, with help

A child can take part in something without doing the whole thing. Both books pair growing participation with an adult who notices what help is needed and remains available.

In the everyday

When your child is interested in joining in or working something out.

Age, interest & context

General guidance for family life with a toddler; no skill is expected at this age.

Look for the part your child is already trying to do. Leave room for that effort, and offer enough help for them to continue. The point is their participation, not finishing every part alone.

During exploration, the books distinguish effort from discouraging frustration. They describe watching, offering help when it is needed, and making space for another attempt.

Davies also describes days when time or patience runs short: the adult can explain that they are helping this time and leave another opportunity for later. Help remains part of the relationship.

What’s underneath

Participation can be small, shared, and supported.
