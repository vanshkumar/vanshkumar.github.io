An everyday situation

Stop the hurt, then understand the moment

Davies keeps an adult close enough to stop hitting, biting, or pushing. Understanding the feeling goes alongside protecting everyone; repair waits until calm.

In the everyday

There has been physical conflict, or you have noticed situations where it is becoming likely.

Age, interest & context

There has been physical conflict, or you have noticed situations where it is becoming likely.

Her first response is to stop the harmful action and move children apart when needed. Acknowledging a feeling does not mean allowing the action to continue.

At a calmer time, notice patterns: what was happening, who was nearby, how noisy or crowded it was, and how the adults responded. Davies offers these as clues for adjusting support, not proof of a child’s motive.

After calm, she helps the child check on the other person and models making amends. Laughter does not change the protective limit or prove why the child acted.
