A small invitation

Two puzzle pieces to try together

When fitting puzzle pieces becomes interesting, Davies suggests showing a turn or offering two jigsaw pieces that fit together. More pieces can come later.

Start by noticing

Your child is trying to fit pieces together and a knob puzzle may no longer offer enough challenge.

Age, interest & context

In the book: The chapter moves from puzzles from about 18 months to jigsaws as a next step, without giving a separate jigsaw starting age.

Interest in fitting shapes together; choose difficulty by the number of pieces and offer two fitting pieces or a demonstration.

A jigsaw adds a different question from a knob puzzle: how do the pieces join? Davies describes children trying the shapes spatially, rather than starting with corners and edges as adults often do.

You can show how two pieces fit, then leave them for your child to explore. Help with a turn if needed, or return to an easier puzzle. Supervise small parts.
