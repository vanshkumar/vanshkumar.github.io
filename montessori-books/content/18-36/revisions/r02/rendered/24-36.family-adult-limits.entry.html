A perspective

Notice your own limit before it builds

Davies suggests noticing an approaching limit and expressing it kindly before irritation builds. The Baby authors also invite reflection on the adult’s own needs, without turning it into a judgment of their parenting.

In the everyday

When a familiar situation leaves you increasingly tired, frustrated, or stretched.

Notice what you are feeling and what you need, as well as what your child needs. Davies describes adult limits as part of balancing the people in a family, and says that following the child leaves room for the adult too.

If you can only make sense of a difficult response later, both books make room for that. The Baby authors suggest reflecting on what drove a reaction and what might have helped; Davies includes thinking or writing later, with compassion.

Davies’s appendix offers feelings and needs vocabulary contributed by Yoram Mosenzon. For example, noticing tiredness and a need for rest can give you more to work with than blaming another person. The table is an aid to reflection, not a test of what you ought to feel.

Reflection sits alongside receiving care: both books include asking others for help, and they direct readers toward a doctor for depression.
