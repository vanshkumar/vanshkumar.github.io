A perspective

Enjoy a new skill without a demonstration

Both books suggest noticing what a child chooses to do instead of asking them to perform a new skill on cue. Naming what interests them and sharing curiosity can keep the exchange going.

In the everyday

When you want to show someone what your child has been trying.

Age, interest & context

Guidance for the adult’s response to a child’s discoveries; no skill demonstration is expected.

Davies recalls asking her son to demonstrate things for relatives, then deciding to stop these tests and watch for what he showed in his own time. Her story offers a way to enjoy a child’s discoveries without needing a performance.

In The Montessori Toddler, she distinguishes a test with one expected answer from a question that invites curiosity. She makes an exception when she is certain the child knows an answer and is excited to share it, which she describes as usually starting around three. The child’s interest matters in that example.
