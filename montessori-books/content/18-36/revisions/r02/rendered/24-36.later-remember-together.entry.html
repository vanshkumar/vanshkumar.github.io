A small invitation

Remember something you did together

When your child is using some spoken language, bring a shared experience into an ordinary conversation. Leave time for the part they want to tell.

Start by noticing

Your child uses some spoken language and has an experience you can remember together.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Around 2 years; the child needs some spoken language.

Some spoken language, with a shared real experience to talk about naturally.

While preparing food or doing the laundry, you might remember planting something together and wonder where you put it or what you used. Davies describes this as a natural conversation, rather than a round of questions to answer correctly.

Listen without finishing the sentence for them. If their meaning is unclear, invite them to show you. Naming objects for a child is different from testing whether they know the names; the book reserves the formal naming test for children over 3.
