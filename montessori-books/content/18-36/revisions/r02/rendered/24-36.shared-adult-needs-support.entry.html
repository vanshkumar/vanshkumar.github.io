A perspective

Rest and support for you

Both books include food, rest, and support for the adult as part of family life. They describe asking for help when depleted, and seeking a doctor’s help for depression.

In the everyday

When looking after everyone else is leaving little room for you.

Age, interest & context

General guidance for family life with a toddler; no skill is expected at this age. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

The authors describe practical help as well as emotional company. A trusted friend, relative, partner if you have one, or another caregiver may be able to share some of the work. Paid help is one possibility when it is accessible.

In The Montessori Baby, help can mean a meal or a household task so you can spend time with your baby. The authors suggest a visible list of useful jobs for visitors who want to help, especially when you are too tired to work out what to ask for.

The Baby authors direct readers struggling with postpartum depression toward a doctor or trusted health professional. Davies also describes getting a doctor’s help when feeling depressed. These help-seeking passages sit alongside the books’ ordinary ideas for rest and support.

What’s underneath

Care for the adult is part of caring for the family.
