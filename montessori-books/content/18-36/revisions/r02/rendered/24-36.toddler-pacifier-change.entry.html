When your family considers a pacifier change

When discussing a young toddler’s pacifier, Davies describes telling the child that a change is coming and giving extra support. The discussion is her approach, not a deadline for this age.

In the everyday

Age, interest & context

In the book: Davies discusses a young toddler who still uses a pacifier.

A family may be considering a change and wondering how to include the child.

This family-change perspective sets no deadline for a 2–3-year-old. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

The book describes a gradual move toward less use and asks the adult to notice whether the child is seeking engagement, closeness, or help settling. Those possibilities are invitations to observe, not certain explanations.

Davies also describes planning the later bedtime change with the child. Her sleep chapter retains support rather than crying alone, and specialist help when persistent difficulties affect the child or family.
