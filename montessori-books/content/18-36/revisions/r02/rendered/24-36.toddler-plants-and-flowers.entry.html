A perspective

Caring for plants and flowers

Davies describes caring for plants and arranging flowers with adult help and manageable tools. Her watering example requires steady walking; her flower example uses stems already cut to length.

In the everyday

Notice an interest in leaves, flowers, carrying a little water, or the work you do for a plant.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Watering is 14+ months with steady walking; flower arranging and germinating seeds are 18+ months in the appendix.

Steady walking for the watering example; interest in plant care or part of arranging flowers; cut stems and supervision retained.

Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 48–52.

Her watering row specifically includes steady walking. Leaf care is another possibility: she describes a small sponge or plant duster. She also includes growing vegetables in pots for homes without a garden.

For flowers, the book uses stems already cut to length, small vases, a little jug and funnel, and a sponge for spills. Your child may be interested only in pouring or wiping at first; the complete arrangement can wait.

Her gardening examples include planting and ongoing care. They are examples of shared work, with supervision for small parts and sharp tools, rather than a requirement to maintain a garden.
