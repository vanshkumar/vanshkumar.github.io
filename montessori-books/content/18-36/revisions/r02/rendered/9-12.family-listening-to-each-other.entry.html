A small invitation

Take turns hearing each other out

For an everyday disagreement with a trusted caregiver who is willing to talk, Davies offers a listening exercise: hear their concern, reflect back what you understood, invite correction, then switch roles.

Start by noticing

When you both want to understand a disagreement but keep talking past each other.

Davies adapts an exercise from Scilla Elworthy’s 2017 Montessori Congress address. She proposes asking whether the other person has twenty minutes: five for them to speak, five for you to reflect what you heard, then the same two turns with the roles reversed.

Reflect both the words and the feelings you think you heard. The speaker can correct a misunderstanding. Naming another person’s feelings is an invitation to check your understanding, not deciding for them.

Davies pairs the exercise with observations, statements about your own feelings and needs, and requests that leave room for other solutions. The Baby authors similarly suggest agreeing on a time to talk; if tiredness or strong feelings make that difficult, they describe proposing a specific later time and returning to it.
