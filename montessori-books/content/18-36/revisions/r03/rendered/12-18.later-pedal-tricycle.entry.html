Moving along at their own pace

Davies describes a low trike or balance bike moved along with the feet, with a pedal tricycle as a separate, later possibility. Interest and what the child can manage guide the choice.

In the everyday

Your child is interested in riding and a different way of moving may be appealing.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Appendix: foot-propelled riding at 14–16 months, pedal tricycles from 2½ years. Balance-bike photograph: from around 2 years, once tall enough.

Interest and manageable movement; the photographed balance bike also requires the child to be tall enough. The book does not give a separate pedal-readiness test.

The appendix’s 14–16 month foot-propelled examples fall within this band. The pictured balance bike and pedal tricycle have later labels; neither is expected here.

Her appendix groups foot-propelled riding at 14–16 months. The photographed balance-bike example has a different label: from around 2 years, once the child is tall enough. Those are separate presentations, not a deadline to combine.

In the balance-bike example, the child pushes with their feet and gradually explores coasting at their own pace. Davies describes a later move to a regular bike as something that often happens, not a promised result.

The appendix introduces pedal tricycles from 2½ years. Pedaling is a different movement from pushing along with the feet; an age label does not replace watching what the child can manage.

Riding is one of many movement possibilities alongside running, dancing and ball play. The examples are not a collection every family needs to buy.
