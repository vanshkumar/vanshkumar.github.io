A perspective

A turn washing or drying dishes

Davies describes washing and drying dishes as supervised work to share, with a small brush or sponge and help ready. Your child can join one part before taking on a longer sequence.

In the everyday

Your child is interested in washing, squeezing a sponge, or using a cloth during cleanup.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Around 2+ years in the appendix; the chapter discusses shared dishwashing without a separate starting age.

Interest in part of the work; adult supervision and more help for a shorter sequence.

Her washing example uses two tubs on a table, a small-handled brush or sponge, a transparent plastic jug, and a travel-sized bottle holding only a little dishwashing liquid. An apron, drying mitt, and hand cloth are nearby.

For drying, she lays a cloth on the table, puts the bowl or glass on it, folds the cloth into it, presses, and unfolds. Show a slow movement and help with carrying; her general guidance uses two hands for a glass.

Keep the work small enough to enjoy together. It may take longer or end after one part; the aim here is participation, not a finished pile of dishes.
