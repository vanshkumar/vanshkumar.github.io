A perspective

Find the hole a bolt fits

Davies’s later nuts-and-bolts example begins with fitting bolts into holes of different sizes. Turning the matching nuts adds another movement, with adult supervision for small parts.

In the everyday

Your child wants to compare sizes, fit objects, or turn a nut while holding a bolt.

Age, interest & context

In the book: From around 2 years in the illustrated example; 2+ years in the appendix.

Start by placing bolts in matching holes; turning nuts is a later part of this same material.

Her example uses a wooden board with differently sized holes and a container of matching nuts and bolts. The child can begin by finding which bolt fits which hole.

As the turning becomes interesting, one hand can hold the bolt while the other turns the nut. Organizing the pieces by size is another possibility; matching every piece is not required.
