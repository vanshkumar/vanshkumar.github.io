Specific words can share your interest: a line on the page, a careful movement, or the help your child offered. Davies also makes room for your own delight.

Age, interest & context

Your child looks to you after doing something, or you want to acknowledge their effort.

Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

The book suggests describing the action instead of giving it a general grade. A warm “I’m excited for you” is among Davies’s examples; this is not a rule to hide affection.

She distinguishes an action from a fixed identity. Describing what happened today leaves more room than calling a child “the clever one,” “the clumsy one,” or “the shy one.”

During absorbed activity, you can wait until the child seeks a response rather than interrupting every step.
