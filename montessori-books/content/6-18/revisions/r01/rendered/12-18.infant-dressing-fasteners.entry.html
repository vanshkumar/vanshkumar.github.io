A perspective

Explore a fastening before getting dressed

Davies’s dressing frames let a child repeat one fastening movement separately from putting on clothes. Help stays available, and the frames are examples rather than a required collection.

In the everyday

Your child may return to pulling apart Velcro or moving a zipper.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Velcro 14+ months; attached zipper 16–18+ months; buttons/snaps 18–22+ months; buckles 2½+ years.

Interest in a particular fastening; manageable challenge and help, without a required sequence.

The earlier variant may be relevant in the later part of this band; later examples keep their own source ages.

The early appendix examples use two pieces of fabric on a wooden frame: Velcro from 14+ months, and a zipper from 16–18+ months. In the zipper example, the fabric stays joined at the bottom.

Her later frames use large buttons or snap fasteners from 18–22+ months and buckles from 2½ years. Those labels describe different possibilities, not a sequence every child must complete.

Show slowly if the child wants help and let them repeat the part that interests them. Small parts require supervision. Managing a fastening during play does not remove the help a child may want while dressing.
