A perspective

A limit you can explain and keep

Davies favors a few clear limits connected to safety and respectful life together. The adult can keep the limit while understanding the child’s disappointment.

In the everyday

You may notice a rule changing from one moment to the next, or realize a limit needs to be clearer.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age; limits are to fit the toddler’s age and ability.

Safety, belongings, shared space or inconsistent rules need clear adult responsibility.

Her examples focus on protecting people, belongings, and shared spaces. A response stays connected to what happened, such as putting away a ball that continues to be thrown indoors and trying again later.

Consistency leaves room for thought. Davies suggests revisiting house rules at a neutral time as children grow; if an adult changes their mind in the moment, they can acknowledge it.

Her checklist asks whether the limit is clear, kind, reasonable, and suited to the child’s age and ability, and whether the child can help find a solution.
