A familiar order to the day

A recognizable sequence can help your child know what comes next. Davies allows the rhythm to follow the child and the family, without keeping every minute fixed.

In the everyday

Transitions may be harder when the day unfolds differently from what your child expects.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age; the book gives a toddler rhythm and a picture-checklist example.

A familiar sequence can help explain what comes next; the adult supports use of pictures.

A picture list is something to look at together, not a task the child must manage independently.

Look at the order your days already tend to follow. A few pictures of the morning or bedtime steps can help you and your child look together at what comes next.

Davies suggests making that picture list with the child. Drawings or photographs can work, and movable pictures can leave room to vary the order.

Her example day is one family rhythm, not a timetable. When a day changes, she suggests anticipating that the difference may be difficult and offering preparation and understanding.
