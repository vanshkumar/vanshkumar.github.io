A perspective

Pressing, sticking, rolling

Davies describes glue, clay, and play dough as chances to explore how materials change. These are shared, supervised explorations, with no model to copy.

In the everyday

Notice an interest in pressing shapes down or changing a material with the hands.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Clay/play dough: from 16 months in the caption, around 16–18+ months in the appendix. Gluing: around 18 months.

Interest in pressing, rolling or changing a material with the hands, with supervision.

The clay example can be relevant toward the later part of this band. Gluing is the separate later example, around 18 months.

Her gluing example uses paper shapes with a small pot of glue and brush, or a glue stick. The movement is to apply glue, turn the shape, and press it onto paper; the appendix uses a few large shapes.

For clay or play dough, she describes flattening, rolling, making a ball, and trying simple tools. She also names kinetic sand as another material experience. Small parts and sharp tools require supervision.

The activity is the experimenting. A child may repeat one movement and leave the rest for another time.
