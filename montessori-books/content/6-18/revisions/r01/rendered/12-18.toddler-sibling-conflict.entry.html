An everyday situation

Help both children with the same problem

When siblings disagree, Davies supports both children without choosing a side. Staying neutral still includes stopping harm and giving as much help as the situation needs.

In the everyday

Two children want the same object, or their play is becoming uncomfortable for one of them.

Age, interest & context

In the book: The sibling section explicitly includes preverbal children and young toddlers; its anecdote has children aged 2 years and 9 months.

Siblings want different things; the adult supports each and stops harm.

The older-child anecdote is not a requirement to negotiate verbally or settle a conflict alone.

Describe what each child seems to want and the problem between them. A young toddler may communicate with actions, and the adult may offer most of the possible solutions.

Small disagreements may need your visible presence; a situation becoming unsafe needs action. Davies describes separating children to prevent harm, then helping them work on a solution after calm.

Her wider sibling discussion favors responding to each child’s needs rather than making every amount of time identical. Shared enjoyable moments can be welcome without requiring siblings to be close friends.
