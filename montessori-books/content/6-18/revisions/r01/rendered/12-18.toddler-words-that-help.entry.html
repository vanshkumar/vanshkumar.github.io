An everyday situation

A few words, then time to respond

A short, useful cue can be easier to act on than repeated instructions. Give your child time, and show what you mean if the words are not enough.

In the everyday

You have asked more than once, and your child has not yet begun.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age; the passage is about communicating with toddlers.

A request has not led to action; the adult can make the cue clearer and show what it means.

Davies suggests a brief piece of information or a familiar word, such as pointing out where something belongs. Go over and show the place if your child seems unsure.

A pause can leave room for processing. She also suggests saying what the child can do, rather than repeatedly describing what to stop.

For a necessary transition, she states what is happening kindly instead of asking a question with no real choice. Gentle humor is another possibility, such as pretending a child’s shoe fits your foot. None of these needs to work every time.
