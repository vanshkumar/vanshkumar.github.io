A perspective

Talk about a recurring night need during the day

Davies suggests returning to repeated nighttime requests at a calm daytime moment. The adult and child can think together about what might help, while keeping the child’s need for support in view.

In the everyday

The same request for a cuddle, drink, cover adjustment or familiar toy keeps coming up at night.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age for the conversation; the book addresses toddlers around 1–3 years and its problem-solving passage includes preverbal children.

Recurring nighttime requests that can be considered at a neutral daytime moment.

An earlier starting example can remain interesting. The source does not require moving to a harder variant because of the browse age.

The conversation happens at a neutral time, when the family can notice what was difficult and consider an idea together. In her wider problem-solving approach, even a preverbal toddler can contribute through an action or gesture; the adult may supply most possibilities.

This does not mean the child must manage the night alone. Davies’s sleep chapter keeps the help the child needs, rejects leaving a child to cry alone, and makes room for extra support during illness or teething.

If continuing sleep difficulties affect the well-being of the child or family, Davies advises seeing a sleep specialist.
