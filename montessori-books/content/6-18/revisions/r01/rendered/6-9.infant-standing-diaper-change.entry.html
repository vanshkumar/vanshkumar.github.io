A perspective

When a standing child resists lying down for a change

For a child who already pulls up to standing and resists lying down, the authors describe a standing diaper change with the adult seated low. Their emphasis remains gentle, private care and participation.

In the everyday

Your child already pulls up and wants to stay standing when a diaper needs changing.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No numerical age is given; the diapering passage says once the baby is pulling up to standing.

Already pulling up to standing and resisting lying down for a needed diaper change.

In the home chapter, the adult sits on a low stool while the child stands for the change, if the space allows. The later diapering chapter connects this possibility with a child who may feel vulnerable when laid down.

The adult still explains what is happening and provides the cleaning. For a bowel movement, the book’s example has the child lean forward with their hands on the side of the bathtub; the same passage describes front-to-back wiping.

The authors keep other possibilities in view: a wish to finish playing, choose something or take more of a part. A standing change does not explain every protest. If necessary care still meets resistance, they describe acknowledging the child’s feelings and proceeding with gentle hands.
