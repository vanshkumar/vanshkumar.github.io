An everyday situation

Time to watch before joining

Let your child stay near you and look around when a social setting feels unfamiliar. Davies offers support without trying to change a quieter temperament.

In the everyday

They may watch from beside you instead of going straight into the group.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age; these are ways to support a toddler in an unfamiliar social setting.

The child wants to watch near the adult before joining.

Verbal greetings and assertive phrases are examples to grow toward, not requirements for a child without those words. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

Beforehand, describe a little of what will happen. On arrival, show them around or allow time at your side while you carry on an ordinary conversation.

Davies suggests small supported practice when it is useful, such as trying a greeting or words for “stop.” The purpose is helping the child handle a situation, not comparing them with a more outgoing child.

An early arrival or shorter visit is another possibility when a busy group is difficult.
