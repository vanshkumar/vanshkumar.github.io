A perspective

Mealtimes as shared time

Davies describes meals as connection as well as nourishment, with the adult arranging what is offered and the child participating at their own pace. She also says food concerns belong with a doctor.

In the everyday

Meals may have become tense, or you may be wondering how much your toddler can join.

Age, interest & context

In the book: The eating chapter addresses toddlers; its milk/glass discussion separately names 12 months.

Shared meals, gradually supported participation, or concerns about how meals are going.

This is Davies’s toddler-mealtime perspective, not a food-intake rule or a requirement for independent eating. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

In her approach, adults decide what, when, and where food is offered, while the child chooses how much to eat. She favors sitting together when feasible, including a small meal for an adult whose own dinner is later.

She does not expect a toddler to remain until everyone finishes. Her examples keep food and cutlery at the table and allow an adult to help with the parts of eating the child cannot yet manage.

Davies asks readers to consider medical or other food-related reasons for refusing food and to consult a doctor for any concern.
