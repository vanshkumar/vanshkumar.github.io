A perspective

Toileting without a race

Davies describes learning to use the toilet as gradual and led by readiness, without a set age or forcing. She includes a doctor’s check when a medical issue is suspected.

In the everyday

Your child may pull at a wet or soiled diaper, seek privacy while pooping, or tell you they have peed or pooped.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Davies explicitly gives no fixed age for toileting readiness.

Possible signs include pulling at a wet diaper, seeking privacy, or communicating about pee or poop.

The toddler placement offers readiness context; it is not an expectation to use the toilet at 12–18 months. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

Davies includes these observations among possible signs of readiness, without setting a deadline. Managing clothes is one of the smaller skills the book describes along the way.

Davies prepares familiar places for clean and soiled clothes and uses matter-of-fact language when a child is wet. Her offers of the potty remain qualified by not interrupting absorbed play and not forcing the child to use it.

The source also discusses overnight changes and stool withholding. Those passages are separate from readiness; she says to check with a doctor if a medical issue is suspected.
