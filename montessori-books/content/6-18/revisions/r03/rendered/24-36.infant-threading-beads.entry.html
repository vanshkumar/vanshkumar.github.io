A perspective

Two hands working on a bead

Davies describes threading as a way to explore what two hands can do together. Bead size and thread thickness follow the child’s ability, with an adult supervising small parts.

In the everyday

Your child may be interested in guiding a thread through a bead and finding it on the other side.

Age, interest & context

In the book: From 16 months in the caption;16–18+ months in the appendix.

Interest in threading; size and thread thickness depend on the child’s ability.

An earlier starting example can remain interesting. The source does not require moving to a harder variant because of the browse age. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

Her illustrated threading example is from 16 months. In the appendix, the 16–18+ month row begins with five or six wooden beads and plastic tubing that can be pushed a little way through a bead.

Her chapter also describes a lace with a firm wooden end before an ordinary shoelace. These are different material examples; choose a manageable challenge rather than trying to follow every variation.

A child can repeat one small part, accept a slow demonstration, or leave it for another time. The later examples with small beads and thin thread are not a requirement to make this activity harder.
