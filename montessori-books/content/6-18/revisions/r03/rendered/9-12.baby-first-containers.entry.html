A perspective

Taking things out of a container

The authors describe taking an object out of a container before learning to place it back. Their main examples are grouped at 9–12 months, with some earlier appendix ages, and require mouthing-safe pieces with secure parts.

In the everyday

Your baby is interested in removing objects, releasing them, or finding where they fit.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Main discussion: 9–12 months; appendix egg/cube between 7 and 9+ months, tray box around 8+, rocking rings once sitting stably around 8–11; stable rings 8–12 dependent on preceding skill.

Interest in removing, intentionally releasing and later replacing objects; stable independent sitting for the seated ring activity.

The activity may continue while it fits your child’s interest and current skills; the source ages are guides.

A wooden egg in a matching cup or a cube in a box gives a baby something to remove and, later, return. A posting box adds a different experience: a ball goes through a hole and reappears in its tray.

Rings may first be taken off a holder before being put back. The appendix’s rocking-base example requires stable sitting; the later stable-base version builds on the preceding skill.

The main discussion groups these examples at 9–12 months, while the appendix starts some at 7–9+ or around 8+ months. These are possibilities to revisit as interest and control grow, not a set to complete at this age.
