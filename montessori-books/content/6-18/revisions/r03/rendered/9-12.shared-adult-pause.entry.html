A perspective

A moment before responding

Both books describe a brief pause before reacting when the child is not in danger. The pause makes room to notice the situation and choose a response; immediate danger is an exception.

In the everyday

When you notice a strong reaction rising in an ordinary moment.

The Baby authors describe acknowledging a crying baby, noticing their need, and responding with presence or comfort as needed. Their pause is part of remaining responsive.

Davies also describes pausing before reacting and expressly excludes immediate danger. Neither book’s counting examples need to become a fixed delay.

Later, the books invite reflection on your own feelings, reactions, and needs. This is a way to understand what happened and what might help another time.

What’s underneath

Notice your own response while staying responsive to the child.
