A perspective

Your baby’s daily rhythm

The authors look for a baby’s changing rhythm of feeding, play and sleep, with familiar cues for transitions. Their pattern is a guide to noticing, not a timetable.

In the everyday

You are trying to understand what your baby needs next.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Starts with newborn rhythms and explicitly continues as the child becomes a young toddler.

The baby’s current feeding, play, sleep and family transitions.

Their usual wake–feed–play–sleep sequence varies as the baby grows. Sometimes play comes before a feed, and travel or changes at home can shift what is familiar.

A repeated phrase or song can signal what is coming. The authors suggest coordinating with other caregivers where possible, while keeping the baby’s own cues in view.

Family rituals can be part of that familiar world too: a regular park visit, a special breakfast or a celebration from one or more family cultures. The baby can be included before they can contribute much themselves.
