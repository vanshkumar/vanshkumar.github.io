An everyday situation

When your baby hits, bites, pushes or throws

When a baby hits, bites, pushes or throws, the authors look for what the movement might communicate while keeping a kind, clear limit on hurting. The adult helps when feelings are too strong for words.

In the everyday

Your baby is showing a strong preference through their movements, and someone or something may be hurt.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Some babies show strong preferences from birth; this particular changing-behavior discussion gives around nine to twelve months and looks ahead to toddler participation in repair.

A baby communicates strong preferences through hitting, biting, pushing or throwing; the adult must keep people and surroundings safe.

The book’s nine-to-twelve-month example can remain useful in the transition to toddlerhood; repair begins with the adult’s example.

The book describes some babies showing stronger preferences around nine to twelve months. A hit might follow unwanted handling; a push might mean wanting to get past. A bite might come with hunger, upset or teething. These are questions to consider, not certain explanations.

Throwing can be a different kind of exploration: the object may be too easy or difficult, or the baby may be interested in how it falls. The authors look for another way to explore that is not dangerous or disruptive. Their boundaries chapter gives a basket of suitable balls as one alternative to throwing an unsuitable object.

The adult stops hurtful actions and can move with the baby away from the situation when needed. During intense crying, the authors use few words, offer a cuddle if wanted and stay nearby if touch is refused.

After calm returns, they give a short explanation and model repair, such as offering a tissue to someone who was hurt or putting thrown things back. They describe babies watching this before taking more of a part as toddlers.

For repeated situations, the book looks at what happened just before, including noise, crowding, a difficult transition or an activity’s fit. Adjusting those conditions is another way to help.
