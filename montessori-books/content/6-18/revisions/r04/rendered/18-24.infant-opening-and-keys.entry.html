A perspective

What makes this open?

Opening and closing can hold a child’s attention all by itself. The books describe a few closures to explore, with an adult choosing and supervising appropriately sized, mouthing-suitable materials with secure parts.

In the everyday

Your child may want another turn with a lid, clasp or turning key.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Opening-and-closing baskets: 12+ months. Separate locks and keys: around 13+. Baby’s furniture-key example has an earlier 10+ label.

Interest in a particular opening movement, with adult help and supervision of the chosen materials.

A familiar lid or closure may remain interesting; the child can repeat just the part they can manage.

Both appendices describe a basket of two or three opening-and-closing objects from 12+ months. A box, tin or purse offers its own movement; there is no need to collect every type.

Keys are a separate example. The Baby appendix describes a key attached by string to lockable furniture from 10+ months. Its separate lock-and-key basket begins around 13+ months. The Toddler appendix’s around-13+ row instead describes a lock and a key strongly attached to a string; its chapter also describes lockboxes.

Turning a key, releasing a clasp and lifting a lid are different problems for the hands. A slow demonstration or adult help can make one part accessible without requiring the child to manage every step.

The Baby authors separately consider finishes suitable for tasting and chewing, the size of each object and its components, and secure attachments without choking hazards.

Stay with the child when materials have small parts. Repeat an interesting movement, simplify the choice, or leave it for another time. These book examples do not require a new collection or a homemade lock board.
