A perspective

Learning to use a fork, spoon and glass

The books describe letting a child take over parts of eating as coordination grows, with small, manageable tools and adult help. Food and cutlery stay at the table.

In the everyday

Your child is interested in bringing a utensil or glass to their mouth during a meal.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Baby gives a seven-month collaboration example and cites fork videos around eight months; Toddler calls this a young-toddler activity without a numerical starting age. Toddler’s book scope is around one to three years.

Interest in taking part during meals, with help suited to coordination; solids after six months and readiness in the Baby account.

Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

In The Montessori Baby, an adult puts food on a fork and leaves the handle toward the baby, who chooses a hand and lifts it. The adult refills it and waits for the baby to continue. Their examples use short, baby-sized handles and fork prongs that are not too sharp.

For a spoon, both books describe thicker foods such as oatmeal while the child is learning to keep food on it. The Baby book also describes a little water in a small glass, with an adult helping support it as needed. Low-sided bowls make the food easier to reach.

The Baby authors favor real dishes but also acknowledge breakage and give less breakable alternatives while a baby is experimenting with dropping. Material choice alone does not show that a dish or utensil suits this child.

Davies gives a similar fork-and-spoon example for young toddlers. Her toddler guidance keeps food and forks at the table and requires supervision for activities involving sharp objects or small parts. Her eating chapter also directs families with eating concerns to a doctor.

These examples sit within each book’s account of eating at the child’s own pace. The Baby book places solids after six months and signs of readiness, alongside choking precautions and learning first aid; its fork examples are not a deadline for using cutlery.
