A perspective

Make room for feelings and keep the limit

Both books allow a child’s feelings while keeping clear limits on actions that cause harm. Understanding an upset child and protecting them or someone else can happen together.

In the everyday

When your child is upset about a boundary.

The books describe acknowledging the feeling and remaining available, rather than trying to make every difficult feeling disappear.

They also keep the adult responsible for stopping unsafe actions and showing what is acceptable. A child’s disappointment does not make the boundary unkind.

When the meaning of an action is unclear, observation can suggest possibilities. Davies treats putting a possible need into words as a tentative interpretation, not certainty about what the child means.

What’s underneath

A feeling can be accepted while an action has a limit.
