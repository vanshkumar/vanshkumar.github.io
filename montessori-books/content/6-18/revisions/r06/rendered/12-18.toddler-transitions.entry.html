An everyday situation

Help a good moment come to an end

Let your child know a transition is coming and make a small plan for finishing. When you can, wait for a pause in what they are doing.

In the everyday

Leaving the park or stopping a puzzle may be difficult even when the next thing is familiar.

Age, interest & context

In the book: No fixed age; the passage addresses toddlers and says clock-time understanding is still developing.

Stopping or leaving a current activity is difficult; help and preparation depend on the situation.

Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

Davies suggests checking that the child heard, then making the ending concrete: one last turn, or a place to save unfinished work. The child need not understand clock time to begin recognizing this pattern.

A small choice or useful job can offer a way to join the transition. Some children do not want choices; those suggestions can be left aside.

If it is time to go, an adult can acknowledge the disappointment and follow through with gentle help. Agreement is an invitation to participate, not a promise that leaving will feel easy.
