A perspective

One hand holds, the other turns

Davies describes a basic nuts-and-bolts activity with one or two shaped pairs, starting with the nuts already attached. Small parts need adult supervision.

In the everyday

Your child may be interested in holding something steady while turning another part.

Age, interest & context

In the book: 12+ months for the basic shaped pairs; the graded board is from around 2 years.

Interest in holding and turning; a manageable challenge with adult supervision.

An earlier starting example can remain interesting. The source does not require moving to a harder variant because of the browse age. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Toddler, PDF pp. 14.

In this early example, each nut corresponds to the shape of its bolt. The child can explore the turning movement without first finding the right place to start the nut.

Her activity chapter describes one hand holding a bolt while the other turns its nut. Show a small movement slowly when help is welcome, and leave time to repeat it.

The photographed board for grading different sizes is a separate, later example from around 2 years. The basic pair does not call for that whole set.
