A perspective

Hear the message in the crying

The authors treat crying as communication: acknowledge it, stay responsive and offer comfort. Trying to understand the need goes alongside care, not ahead of needed help.

In the everyday

Your baby is crying and you are trying to understand what they need.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Responsive crying care begins from birth; the dedicated care section gives no upper age for responding or seeking help.

The child is crying; care and comfort remain responsive while the adult seeks to understand.

They describe acknowledging a cry and responding with presence or holding when needed. Feelings can be heard without asking the baby to stop crying or insisting that everything is fine.

In their discussion of prolonged crying and pain, the authors include medical advice and sharing observations with a doctor or pediatrician. They also recognize how tiring this can be and the value of support for the adult.
