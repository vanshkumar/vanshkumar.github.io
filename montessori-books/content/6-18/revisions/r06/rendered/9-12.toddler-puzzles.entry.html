A small invitation

Take a puzzle apart, and try a piece

A simple puzzle can be interesting before your child can put it back together. Offer a manageable challenge and help them repeat the part they enjoy.

Start by noticing

Notice whether they are lifting pieces out, turning them, or trying to fit one into the base.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Baby describes mostly taking knobbed puzzle pieces out at 9–12 months; Toddler’s appendix lists puzzles from 12–14+ months.

Interest in taking pieces out, with the adult replacing them for another turn.

Baby’s illustrated inventory places a knobbed puzzle in 6–9 months, while its fuller description places this removal activity at 9–12 and its appendix says 12+. This placement follows the fuller removal description; fitting is not expected. Age and readiness sources: The Montessori Baby, PDF pp. 167–168, 175, 303.

Davies begins with simple knob puzzles. If your child mostly removes the pieces, you can replace them so there is another chance to take them out.

When a piece becomes frustrating, offer to show a slow turn or give a small cue, then see how they continue. A harder puzzle can wait. Supervise any activity with small parts.
