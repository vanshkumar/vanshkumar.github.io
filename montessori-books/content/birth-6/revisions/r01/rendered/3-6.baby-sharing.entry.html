An everyday situation

Sharing toys and taking turns

If another child wants what your baby is using, notice whether your baby seems ready to move on. If your baby seems to want to keep playing, you can explain that wish to the other child.

In the everyday

Two children want the same object.

Age, interest & context

When your baby is holding something another child wants. Understanding sharing is not expected.

The authors notice that a young baby may happily move to something else, or may want to hold on. Respond to what is happening rather than requiring either reaction.

When your baby reaches for another child’s object, the book models asking whether that child is finished. Waiting, watching or finding another object are possibilities while the turn continues.
