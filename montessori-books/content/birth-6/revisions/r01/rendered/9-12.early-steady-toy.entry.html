A perspective

A toy that stays put when touched

The appendix describes a toy on a suction base that rocks when batted but stays in place. The example depends on secure attachment and small parts remaining enclosed.

In the everyday

Your baby is interested in batting or reaching, while objects that move away are harder to return to.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Around 5+ months or once sitting, in the appendix row.

Interested in batting and reaching toward a toy that remains in place; sitting is not imposed for the activity.

The row combines an approximate age with an alternative readiness cue. Choose by current interest, not by this browsing band.

The toy’s fixed base offers a different experience from a rolling object: the baby can reach toward it again without the toy moving across the space. The authors’ example is a clear ball containing smaller balls, attached to a suction stand.

The row says “around 5+ months or once sitting.” The authors’ wider guidance favors activities that fit current interest and movement, and cautions against propping a baby into sitting before they can get there themselves.
