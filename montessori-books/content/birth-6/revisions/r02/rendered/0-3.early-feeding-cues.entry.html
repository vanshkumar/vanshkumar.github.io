A perspective

Learning your baby’s feeding signals

The authors describe learning a baby’s feeding signals by noticing their movements, sounds and responses. The same cry can have different needs behind it, so these signs are possibilities to understand alongside responsive care.

In the everyday

You are getting to know how your baby communicates around milk feeds.

Age, interest & context

For understanding communication around breast or bottle feeds.

Their examples include an opening mouth, whimpering or squeaking, tension in the mouth or body, and beginning to cry. They also ask what the baby looks at and how nearby sounds affect them.

They describe feeding a newborn on demand and gradually learning that baby’s rhythm. Their wider care discussion calls for a prompt response to needs; observation is part of caring, rather than a test the baby must pass before a feed.

In the same feeding section, they acknowledge choices, difficulties and specialist support when feeding is not going easily.
