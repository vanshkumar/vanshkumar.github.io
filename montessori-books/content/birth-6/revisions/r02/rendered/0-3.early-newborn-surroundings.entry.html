A perspective

A gentler introduction to everyday life

The authors suggest simpler surroundings and gentle, unhurried handling in the first weeks. They also recognize that a quiet home is not always possible, especially with siblings.

In the everyday

Your newborn is meeting the sounds, light and touch of daily life.

Age, interest & context

In the book: The first 6 to 8 weeks; some suggestions concern the first days.

Fewer visitors and softer lighting are among their possibilities for the first days, when family circumstances allow. A baby in a busier home is also getting to know the people and rhythms around them.

Their newborn-care discussion notices how much a change of clothes, a bath or a diaper change asks a baby to take in. They describe slowing the handling and keeping movements gentle.

In the interview with Karin Slabaugh, explaining an action before it happens and pausing to notice the baby are ways of showing respect. This attention is an invitation to understand the baby, without making a quiet change the measure of good care.
