A perspective

Connection can be part of feeding

The authors describe breast or bottle feeding as time for closeness and communication. They also acknowledge feeding choices, difficulties and the support a family may need.

In the everyday

Your baby is feeding, and you notice their gaze, sounds or movements.

Holding the baby, meeting their gaze and noticing their response are forms of connection in the book’s account. This possibility belongs to different primary caregivers and feeding arrangements.

The authors acknowledge that some people choose not to breastfeed, that difficulties may call for specialist support, and that feeding not going as hoped can bring grief. The relationship is not limited to one way of offering milk.
