A perspective

When looking becomes reaching

As a baby starts reaching and batting, the authors describe changing from fragile looking mobiles to sturdy tactile materials, with secure attachment and every part suitable for grasping and mouthing.

In the everyday

Your baby studies their hands, reaches toward a mobile or bats at it.

Age, interest & context

In the book: 3 to 6 months; bell and ring appendix rows say around 4+ months; a sitting-mobile example appears at 6 to 9 months.

Interest in the hands, reaching or batting; the seated variant waits for self-initiated sitting.

The dates describe the book’s examples. A change from looking to grasping guides the choice.

A tactile mobile responds to the baby’s own movement. The book contrasts a bell that makes a sound when batted with a ring whose interest is in reaching, grasping and drawing it closer.

The authors require an attachment that withstands pulling, material suitable for the mouth and components that cannot come loose or become choking hazards.

As sitting develops, the book describes a different view: reaching forward rather than upward. Its discussion also cautions against propping a baby into sitting before they can get there themselves.
