A perspective

Sleep with the help they need

The authors begin with this baby’s tired signs and the help they need, in a safe, comfortable sleep setting. A short familiar routine can offer connection; falling asleep is not a performance.

In the everyday

Your baby is winding down or needs support settling.

A few familiar words, a song or a short book are among the connecting moments they describe. The adult notices and responds, while the baby is the one who falls asleep.

They describe gradually changing help when both baby and adult are ready, and explicitly say it is okay if feeding or rocking is currently part of settling. Their broader discussion also acknowledges night waking during the first year.

The book’s sleep-surface recommendations contain tensions with its own safety box; this account concerns responsive support and does not choose a sleeping arrangement.
