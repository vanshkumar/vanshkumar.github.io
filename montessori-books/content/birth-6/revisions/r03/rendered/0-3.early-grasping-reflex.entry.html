A perspective

An early grasp can be a reflex

The authors distinguish a newborn’s reflex grasp from the later choice to reach for an object. These are different moments in their account of hand movement.

Age, interest & context

In the book: First 2 months in the Grasping rattles passage.

Fingers close when something touches the palm; this is described separately from intentional reaching.

The book separates this early reflex from the intentional reaching described later.

In the first two months, the book describes fingers closing around an adult’s finger or an early grasping object. It later describes a baby studying their hands, reaching and batting with growing intention.

The authors distinguish this early grasp from independently using a rattle: recovering a dropped object also calls for movement such as turning or slithering. Their activity ages are guides, and they welcome seeking support for developmental concerns.
