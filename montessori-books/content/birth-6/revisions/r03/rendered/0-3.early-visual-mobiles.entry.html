A perspective

A mobile for quiet looking

The authors describe light, slowly moving mobiles for an awake baby to watch. Their visual mobiles are fragile and meant for looking; reaching and batting call for a different kind of material.

In the everyday

Your baby is interested in watching a slow movement or a changing view.

Age, interest & context

In the book: Early weeks and first 3 months; appendix examples also include around 3+ months.

Interested in looking; reaching and batting call for tactile materials instead.

The book’s examples have different ages. Interest and the kind of interaction matter more than completing a mobile sequence.

The book’s examples include black-and-white shapes, reflective colored octahedrons, shades of one color, and paper figures that seem to dance. The authors also welcome other simple mobiles; the examples are not a set a family needs to acquire.

They place visual mobiles in the awake movement area, separate from the bed. Looking at the mobile from underneath helps an adult consider the baby’s view. They favor gentle movement from air currents and do not require accompanying music.

The authors watch for interest rather than a set viewing time. If the baby looks away, becomes tense or cries, they describe moving the mobile away, changing its position or trying another day. When reaching and batting begin, they distinguish sturdy tactile materials from these fragile looking mobiles.
