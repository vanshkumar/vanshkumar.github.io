A small invitation

Make space for everyone’s needs

Bring the adults’ needs into the family conversation too. Look together at the care, rest, company, and practical work that need room in the coming days.

Start by noticing

When family plans seem to leave someone’s needs out.

Age, interest & context

When care, practical work, and different people’s needs need attention.

This is an adult conversation about family life, with no child skill required.

If you share care, check how the other person is doing and what support would help. The Baby authors suggest a regular chance to discuss arrangements, what is working, and what needs adjusting.

Davies describes planning around different needs, such as errands, rest, friendship, and a child’s wish to play. Her example looks for a workable combination rather than making the child’s enjoyment a reward for being good.

The books recognize many forms of family and support. A partner, relatives, or friends may have a part, according to who is in your life. Start with what is possible; the conversation need not produce a perfect solution.

What’s underneath

The child’s needs belong alongside the needs of the family.
