A perspective

Room for their own movement

The authors favor awake movement that the baby initiates, with room to explore, help when needed, and no propping into sitting or standing before they can get there themselves.

In the everyday

Notice how your baby moves now, what catches their attention, and when effort becomes frustration.

Age, interest & context

In the book: 0 to 3 months and 3 to 6 months; the book discusses awake movement from the early months.

Notice current movement and comfort; the first-month passage also emphasizes holding and snuggling.

The book pairs early awake movement with holding and snuggling. Follow current interest and comfort. Age context: The Montessori Baby, Movement activities / 0 to 3 months and Movement mat, PDF pp. 144–145.

Davies and Uzodike describe a prepared space where a baby can explore their own movements. A nearby object may invite a reach or a change of direction; the point is to follow the baby’s present interest and pace.

Their caution is about propping a baby into sitting or standing. They separately describe awake time on both the back and tummy, while noticing discomfort and changing things or picking the baby up when needed.

When effort turns to frustration, they describe coming closer and offering enough help for the baby to continue. Their activity ages are guides, and they explicitly welcome seeking support for developmental concerns.
