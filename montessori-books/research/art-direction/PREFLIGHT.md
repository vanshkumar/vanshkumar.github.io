## Shared source boundaries

References below use manifest IDs `baby-2021` and `toddler-2019` and **physical PDF
pages**. No Baby printed-page offset is used; no Toddler printed citations are
needed here. Evidence IDs resolve in the books' canonical `EVIDENCE.jsonl` files.

Baby PDF 119–127 and the guideline paragraph on 294 distinguish individual
interest from age, allow quiet watching and partial exploration, and preserve
help for frustration and support-seeking for developmental concerns. Toddler PDF
33–40 similarly qualifies ages, allows help/repetition/partial sequences, discourages
quizzing, and permits household substitutes. These frames apply to the proposals,
not just to accompanying copy. A still image cannot establish readiness, consent,
safety certification, or a developmental outcome.

The figure arrangements and palette are editorial staging. Only the named
interaction and stated conditions are source claims. Use original drawings;
source illustrations are evidence to inspect, never artwork to copy. Show modest
ordinary clothes and surroundings, available adult help, and an engaged child
without requiring a perfect smile or completed result.

## Five primary scenes

### 0–3 months: a quiet exchange in caring arms

**Gesture and composition:** a seated caregiver holds an awake newborn face to
face, the baby's body resting along the adult's forearms and head visibly cradled
in the hands. Show a pause in the exchange: the adult listens, the baby's mouth
slightly open or face attentive, without demanding imitation. The linked arms
and small face form the focal shape at lower center/right, leaving upper-left
paper for the headline. No equipment or toy is needed.

**Evidence:** `baby.ev.newborn-conversation-introduction`, PDF 18, the
face-to-face conversation paragraph, supplies the forearm/head-support setup;
`baby.ev.ch06-conversation-0-3`, PDF 129–130, supplies waiting and responding;
`baby.ev.ch06-newborn-orientation`, PDF 144, supplies early holding/snuggling
context. Read the surrounding units on 17–22, 128–132, and 143–146.

**Age and cues:** PDF 18 explicitly includes newborns; 129 labels its section
0–3 months; 144 specifically describes the first month. The 0–3 browsing placement
fits these sources. Sounds, gestures, and attention guide the exchange; a returned
tongue movement, smile, reduced crying, or sustained eye contact is not required.

**Must not imply:** a newborn should hold their own head, perform on cue, or
complete a timed response. Do not illustrate sleep equipment or turn the adjacent
claims about trust or head strength into a guaranteed outcome or age threshold.

### 3–6 months: held close through a song

**Gesture and composition:** one caregiver stands in a relaxed, grounded stance,
holding the awake baby close while gently swaying to a song. Keep both supporting
arms and the baby's head/body relationship legible; use no sling, music box,
instrument, or loose prop. A tall figure group at right creates a different
silhouette from the seated newborn scene. Preserve headline space at left and
check that the baby remains large enough to read at the actual display size.

**Evidence:** `baby.ev.ch06-language-music-and-conversation`, PDF 131–132,
the held dancing/swaying paragraph and its quiet-listening continuation;
`baby.ev.app-activity-music`, PDF 295, the complete
Music/dance/movement/singing row. PDF 134 also continues singing in the 3–6
language section; `baby.ev.ch06-conversation-3-6` records that section's exchange
context. Read 128–136, the complete activity introduction 119–127, and 294–295.

**Age and cues:** the body example appears under **0–3 months**, while the
appendix row says **all ages** and specifically places singing from birth.
Choosing it for 3–6 months is editorial reuse, not a newly sourced 3–6-only
activity. The source invites music enjoyed by adult and baby, with attentive
listening and quiet between occasions; no dance or vocal response is required.

**Must not imply:** unsupported head/torso, dangling from hands, bouncing or
spinning instruction, a babywearing technique, constant music, hearing assessment,
or promised language improvement. The book gives no technical carrying or dance
protocol: task 8 must review the actual pose, not infer one from the word sway.

### 9–12 months: a face reappears

**Gesture and composition:** an adult seated low at right opens their hands away
from their own face in a small peekaboo exchange. The baby faces them from the
floor at left; an independently seated pose is provisional, subject to the
readiness constraint below. The two faces and opening hands are the focal action.
No cloth covers the baby; no hidden toy, furniture, or elaborate hiding place is
needed. Leave a clear gap between faces and headline space above the low child.

**Evidence:** `baby.ev.ch06-pointing-and-sound-games`, PDF 136, the complete
9–12 month language unit, explicitly names peekaboo. Hands opening from the
adult's own face are editorial staging of the named game, not a procedure given
by the source. Positioning qualification: `baby.ev.ch06-self-initiated-sitting`,
PDF 159, read with 157–159 and the complete illustrated grid on 165–166.

**Age and cues:** 136 states **9–12 months**, with interest in hidden things and
retrieving them. This supports browsing placement, not a test of memory,
pointing, smiling, or finding. If seated, the pictured baby must be understood as
already able to enter/leave that posture, not positioned there to practice it.

**Unresolved constraint:** the sitting record is **held** because the 6–9 prose
and 3–6 illustrated grid differ. This preflight does not resolve that hold. Task 8
must settle the precise posture and nearby readiness wording with the reviewed
entry before using the seated variant; change the pose or candidate if it cannot
be represented faithfully. Do not imply sitting by nine months, propping,
that object permanence begins at nine months, or a compulsory happy reaction.

### 12–18 months: one arm finds a sleeve

**Gesture and composition:** a child sits on the floor beside a kneeling adult.
A loose T-shirt is already over the head; the child works an arm toward the
sleeve while the adult stays available to help. Keep the face uncovered and the
sleeve opening, child hand, and adult hands distinct. This is a close care moment
with one garment, not a dressed-and-ready achievement portrait. Place the group
low/right with uncluttered paper at left.

**Evidence:** `toddler.care.ev.dressing-scaffold`, PDF 150–151, the T-shirt
example, limited help, refusal, and changing wishes for help; read the complete
dressing unit 148–151. `toddler.activities.ev.dressing`, PDF 51, Bedroom/dressing,
and PDF 247, Undressing, dressing, and storing clothes, supply separate age
witnesses. `baby.ev.dressing-participation`, PDF 207–208, supports the earlier
care relationship; its necessary resistance/physical-care context is 209–210.

**Age and cues:** the Toddler chart says **12–18 months, with assistance**;
the broader appendix activity says **14+ months**; the detailed sleeve example
has no numerical age and refers to younger toddlers. Baby gives no numerical
dressing age. The band is an editorial placement for partial participation with
help, not an assertion that every child can find a sleeve at twelve months.

**Must not imply:** restraint, an adult pulling the child's arm through, help
withheld after frustration, compulsory independence, or dressing without adult
care. Keep the source's day-to-day variation and necessary-care limits. Do not
add a dressing stool, mirror installation, hot drink, or full dressing sequence.

### 2–3 years: a little laundry sorted together

**Gesture and composition:** adult and child sit beside an ordinary laundry
basket, working with a few clean socks or clothes. The child moves one item
toward a loose group of the same color; the adult participates alongside them.
Use two clearly differentiated color groups and a remaining unsorted item as
editorial composition, not a required count. Let the basket and scattered fabric
make a low horizontal scene; put the taller adult at right. This is shared
household work, with no table, machine, detergent, or special Montessori material.

**Evidence:** `toddler.activities.ev.sort-laundry`, PDF 49, Helping with the
laundry, and PDF 52, **left-hand** 18-months-to-3-years column, Other/sorting.
Read the full practical-life unit 48–52 and general principles 33–40.

**Age and cues:** the chart says **18 months to 3 years**, not 2–3 specifically.
The chosen band is an editorial subset. There is no row-specific readiness test:
interest, manageable challenge, and help/partial participation come from the
general context. Naming colors or making correct pairs is not a prerequisite.

**Must not imply:** a color quiz, matching assessment, perfect piles, a finished
chore, or a promised lifelong helper. Crucially, show **sorting**, not folding:
the neighboring **right-hand 3–4 years** column lists folding laundry and socks.
No washing-machine operation, detergent handling, or complex household sequence
is supported by this narrow scene brief.

## One optional key moment worth considering

**A little help at the back of a shoe.** If the selected dressing entry needs to
explain proportionate help, a small original close view can show the adult holding
the shoe's back while the child puts their foot in. This makes the division of
action visible in a way a generic seated pair cannot. Basis:
`toddler.care.ev.dressing-scaffold`, PDF 150, with complete 148–151 context;
`toddler.care.ev.manageable-clothes`, PDF 148. No numerical age for that example;
use the actual entry's readiness/context, a manageable shoe, and no forced foot,
lacing lesson, or independence deadline. Skip it if the entry and main scene
already explain enough. No additional image quota is proposed.
