# Editorial charter

Version 1, 2026-09-10. Small Beginnings is a layered, source-faithful companion to
*The Montessori Baby* and *The Montessori Toddler*, from birth through age three.
It includes the adult and family experience. Pregnancy and birth preparation are
outside the approved scope; newborn material within a mixed chapter remains in
scope. This charter applies the approved `POPULATION_PLAN.md`; it is not a claim
that the books themselves prescribe this product structure.

## What the companion helps a reader do

Notice something about their child or situation, understand the authors'
perspective, and find a supported possibility that fits. A useful entry can be a
practical invitation, a perspective with no steps, or help understanding an
everyday situation. It need not produce a finished activity or a measurable result.

The books repeatedly place relationship, observation, and the adult's role beside
materials and activities. Baby introduces collaboration before increasing
independence (PDF pp. 18-21); Toddler describes the adult as a guide and makes clear
that Montessori is more than the materials (p. 8 / PDF p. 17; p. 186 / PDF p. 195).
Keep that balance in selection and copy. Neither a large activity catalog nor a
beautiful room can stand in for the books' adult/family material.

They are not independently reviewed app entries. Attach the relevant local source

## Source fidelity before brevity

Read by source section before synthesizing by age or topic. Account for every
substantive idea in the later coverage ledgers, including ones not made into
entries. Record whether an idea is included, combined with a surviving idea,
retained as context, or excluded for a stated reason. Do not invent entry counts
or treat repeated source ideas as an obligation to repeat reader copy.

Paraphrase the actual passage. Preserve whether it is an author recommendation,
personal practice, anecdote, developmental assertion, attributed third-party idea,
or Montessori principle as the authors explain it. Both books name influences
beyond Montessori (Baby PDF pp. 20-21; Toddler pp. 3, 10 / PDF pp. 12, 19). Do not
attribute every exercise or script directly to Maria Montessori or present it as
independently established science.

Each practical detail, age statement, setup and qualification needs traceable
support. A neighboring chapter heading is not evidence. Read complete lists,
tables, captions and continuation pages; find necessary internal cross-references.
If a detail is absent or ambiguous, record the gap rather than completing the
instruction with generic Montessori knowledge. External linked resources are not
silently added to the two-book source base.

Compression must not strengthen certainty. A possibility must not become a promise;
an example must not become the only way; a question about a child's need must not
become a diagnosis of motive. In particular, record and hold deterministic claims
for explicit disposition rather than laundering them into gentle prose. Baby PDF
pp. 96-97 contains time-limited basic-trust and outcome claims that sit alongside
the book's individual-timeline framing (PDF pp. 100, 119). The approved companion
does not turn them into parenting deadlines or guaranteed outcomes. The ledger
must say what was omitted or qualified and why.

## Age is browsing, with a separate evidence trail

The approved bands are 0-3, 3-6, 6-9, 9-12, 12-18, 18-24 months and 2-3 years.
Keep three things separate in evidence: the book's exact age wording (if any),
the book's interest/readiness/context cues, and the editor's reason for placing
the entry in a band. No numerical age is inferred from a picture or an adjacent
table row. A reader should not meet a hidden developmental test in a title,
illustration, summary, sequence, or topic listing.

Baby PDF p. 119 rejects using the activities to speed or slow individual
development and preserves seeking support for concerns; its appendix opening on
PDF p. 294 says activity ages are guidelines. Toddler p. 24 / PDF p. 33 gives a
parallel age qualification. Keep both the flexibility and any source-stated
support/limits, rather than replacing them with "every child will get there."

A source labeled toddler-wide or all ages retains its original context. Shared
adult principles can recur through canonical reuse; a specific toddler action
cannot be applied to a newborn merely because the general principle also applies.
Sources extending past age three are accounted for as scope/context, not squeezed
into the 2-3-year band.

## A useful, supportive voice

Use concrete observations and ordinary verbs. Offer a clear possibility with a
reason, and name the condition that makes it relevant. Preserve the warmth of
the books without filling space with generic reassurance or implying that a parent
must feel calm before they are allowed to begin. See `TONE_EXAMPLES.md`.

- Describe the child as a participant with interests and feelings. Avoid fixed
  labels such as good/bad, advanced/behind, difficult/easy or successful/failed.
- Explain a limit without casting the child as an adversary. Do not promise that
  the child will agree, calm down, cooperate or learn on a schedule.
- Offer small, feasible participation and help. Respect limited time, energy,
  money, space and support; do not assume a dedicated room, a partner, a birth
  mother at home, or purchasable assistance.
- Include what an adult can notice, adjust, ask for, or repair. The adult is not
  merely a tool for producing a particular child outcome.
- Keep steps optional and source-supported. Perspectives can stop after an
  explanation. No required shopping, prescribed schedules, completion tracking,
  scores, streaks, parent grades or activity quotas.

These choices are grounded in the books' explicit invitations to use available
materials and start modestly (Baby PDF p. 19; Toddler pp. 3, 10 / PDF pp. 12, 19),
their family realities, and their rejection of perfect parenting (Baby PDF
pp. 238-242; Toddler p. 178 / PDF p. 187). They do not require erasing difficulties
or making vague assurances.

## Safety-sensitive source material

Describe the authors' perspective with precise references and retain meaningful
qualifications. This applies to sleep, feeding, positioning/movement, choking and
materials, illness and developmental concerns, adult distress, and family safety
where encountered. Do not add instructions, substitute outside guidance, or claim
independent medical verification. An illustration is also a claim about setup and
action; decorative context must not introduce unsupported sensitive details.

Use attribution where it helps distinguish the book's view, such as "The authors
describe..." or "Davies presents...". Do not convert a sensitive passage into a
new care protocol or an imperative checklist. Source attribution alone does not
repair an omitted caution. If a short version cannot preserve a meaningful
qualification, expand it, retain it as context, or hold it from publication.

The source-note files record concrete traps: observation versus immediate danger,
material preference versus safety claims, adult self-care versus the books'
help-seeking language, and family contact versus explicit safety exceptions.
These are editorial checks on representation, not new advice. The full book
research must read the dedicated sensitive sections before any such content or
artwork is drafted.

## Product and review boundaries

The accepted round 16 composition remains the visual baseline: three curated
opening invitations, an age-specific illustration and one focused reading panel,
with five topics behind Explore more. This foundation task changes no app code
or artwork. Content depth follows the books; the opening selection is later
curation, not a ceiling on coverage.

Reuse canonical shared guidance rather than subtly changing it in seven places.
Keep research, evidence and review states outside reader copy. Every published
entry, including the six prototypes, needs distinct independent xhigh source
and spirit/tone reviews against its exact revision. Unresolved entries remain
unpublished. Foundation examples and investigator notes confer no publication
approval. `EVIDENCE_CONTRACT.md` defines the durable review trail.
