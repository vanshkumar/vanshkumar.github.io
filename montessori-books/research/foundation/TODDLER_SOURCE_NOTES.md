## Ages, readiness, and scope

- Davies defines her use of *toddler* as approximately ages one to three
  (PDF 14 / printed 5). This book alone does not establish a newborn activity's
  suitability. Adult and family themes may be candidates for reuse across the
  companion, but application to younger babies needs the Baby research or an
  explicit, reviewed editorial rationale.
- The activity chapter states that listed ages are indications and asks adults
  to follow interest, removing activities that are too difficult or too easy
  (PDF 33 / printed 24). This is the strongest direct basis here for age as a
  browsing aid. The companion's seven bands are editorial groupings, not seven
  stages supplied by this book.
- The sensitive-period table explicitly places its timing under individual
  variation. Its rows include tiny-detail interest from 18 months to three years,
  manners around 2.5 years with adults modeling beforehand, and approximate
  writing/reading interests outside the companion's age scope. Preserve the
  table-level qualification whenever reusing a row; do not promote these into
  deadlines or tests (PDF 25-26 / printed 16-17).
- The book's particular numerical ages, such as the 18-month-to-three-year
  self-affirmation discussion, remain book-stated framing rather than a promise
  about an individual child (PDF 15 / printed 6). The child-development section
  explicitly allows individual timelines, different energy levels, observation
  as a way to learn, and different amounts of movement (PDF 29 / printed 20).
- Match challenge to what the child is trying, and allow the activity to become
  simpler or more complex. A child may work on one small part before the entire
  sequence (PDF 34-35 / printed 25-26; PDF 39 / printed 30; PDF 101 / printed 92).
- The book's account of the absorbent mind explicitly includes birth to three
  (PDF 27 / printed 18). Treat this as Montessori framing, not independent proof
  that every experience has a particular measurable developmental effect.

## Safety-sensitive editorial boundaries

These are boundaries for faithful representation of the source, not additional
care instructions or claims of clinical verification.

- **Exploration and observation do not remove supervision.** The general
  activity principles explicitly require supervision for small parts and sharp
  objects (PDF 39 / printed 30). A reusable-material suggestion must not lose
  this caution when separated from the page. The source's wooden-material
  preference is not a blanket safety approval for any wooden object
  (PDF 34 / printed 25).
- **Pausing has an immediate-danger exception.** The adult's suggestion to pause
  before reacting expressly excludes immediate danger (PDF 194 / printed 185).
  Observation also helps identify when to step in for safety (PDF 105 / printed
  96). Do not compress this into an unconditional wait or a claim that help is
  available only when the child requests it.
- **Trust in development is not a clinical assurance.** The curiosity chapter's
  trust discussion is followed by physical and emotional safety responsibilities
  (PDF 95-96 / printed 86-87). Later, Davies supports researching a child's
  differences and getting needed support (PDF 189 / printed 180). Do not turn
  the first passage into a claim that an injury is harmless, that differences
  never need attention, or that developmental concerns should be ignored.
- **Home independence retains hazard and mixed-age context.** The home chapter
  pairs accessibility with removing unsuitable items, making at least one safe
  area, and keeping older children's small parts away from younger children
  (PDF 83-84 / printed 74-75). The small-space paragraph also mentions high beds
  without a toddler-specific age qualification (PDF 85 / printed 76). This
  foundation read does not authorize turning that example into a birth-to-three
  sleeping recommendation; hold such particulars for source research and review.
- **Adult care includes outside help.** Davies explicitly directs an adult who
  feels depressed to seek a doctor's help (PDF 188 / printed 179). Preserve this
  if summarizing that section; do not substitute a ritual, mindfulness exercise,
  or app activity for it. These notes provide no diagnosis or treatment protocol.
- **Family-contact language has a safety exception.** The separation section's
  support for both parents' involvement is qualified by psychological or physical
  reasons against contact and an explicit child-safety priority (PDF 210 /
  printed 201). Its ordinary conflict exercise is not evidence for requiring
  reconciliation or contact in every situation (PDF 209-211 / printed 200-202).
- **Illustrative reframing is not proof of a motive.** The introductory infographic
  gives emphatic positive interpretations of toddler behavior (PDF 18 / printed
  9). Read with the nonjudgmental-observation guidance, it supports changing an
  adult's perspective, not asserting the cause of every tantrum or night waking
  (PDF 105 / printed 96). Keep that distinction in editorial synthesis.
- **No procedure follows from this foundation read alone.** Feeding, sleep,
  toileting, screen time, hitting/biting, and specific outdoor or practical-life
  activities have not been researched here. Their headings in the contents are
  navigation only; later records need their actual pages, qualifications,
  cross-references, and independent reviews.
