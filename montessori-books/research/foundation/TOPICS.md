# Topic definitions

These five labels are the approved product's browsing structure, not a claim that
the books use this taxonomy. Assign a primary topic by the reader's purpose; add
secondary links only where they help retrieval. One canonical entry can appear
under several ages or topics without being rewritten.

| Stable key / reader label | Include | Boundary and source anchors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `play-discovery` / **Play & discovery** | Invitations to explore, concentrate, communicate, move, make, listen, observe nature, and take part in purposeful activity. | Ground in Baby ch. 6 and Toddler ch. 3. These are broader than toys or formal lessons. A sourced everyday task can belong here when the focus is exploration/participation; when the focus is managing care in daily life, use Everyday care. |
| `everyday-care` / **Everyday care** | Daily rhythms and rituals, dressing, eating, sleeping, washing, diapering/toileting, and related transitions or everyday situations. | Ground in Baby ch. 7 and Toddler ch. 7. Preserve the source's context and cautions. Sensitive care content is descriptive of the authors' perspective; a care topic is not permission to convert it into a protocol. |
| `connection-feelings` / **Connection & feelings** | Listening and responding, relationships, attachment framing, acceptance, emotional expression, cooperation, kind limits, conflict between children, and reconnection. | Ground in Baby ch. 5 and ch. 7 common questions; Toddler chs. 5-6 and ch. 7 useful skills. Feelings may be accepted while actions have limits. Avoid treating every distressed child as a behavior problem or promising cooperation. |
| `home-that-helps` / **A home that helps** | Accessible, orderly, purposeful spaces; choosing, arranging, storing and rotating what is available; shared/small spaces and room adaptations. | Ground in both ch. 4s and prepared-environment principles. Describe the purpose and feasible setup; do not turn home tours into required purchases or an aesthetic standard. A specific activity's setup stays with that activity unless the environment idea stands alone. |
| `you-family` / **For you & your family** | Adult observation/reflection, presence, mistakes and repair, rest and support, partners/co-parents/single parents, siblings in the family system, other caregivers, and working together. | Ground in both chs. 8-9 and Baby ch. 7 other situations. Adult needs are substantive content, not a generic reassurance footer. Keep differences, practical constraints, and source qualifications visible. |

Chapter anchors above are orientation from the contents and foundation reading;
they are not evidence for an unresearched specific claim. Later evidence records
must name actual passages. See the two source-note files for read spans and
qualifications, and `SOURCE_MANIFEST.md` for pagination.

## How to decide overlapping cases

- **A child joining a familiar task:** Play & discovery when the invitation is to
  take part and explore a skill; Everyday care when the entry addresses the daily
  care situation. Use secondary discovery links for the same canonical guidance.
- **Caregiver announcing what will happen during care:** Connection & feelings
  when respectful interaction is the subject; Everyday care when embedded in the
  larger care situation. Keep supported interaction details in both contexts by
  reuse rather than competing scripts.
- **A low, accessible place for materials:** A home that helps for the reusable
  environmental idea; activity-specific material placement can remain in the
  relevant invitation. Do not infer exact dimensions or required furnishings.
- **Observation:** the charter's reusable principle can inform every topic. An
  adult reflection entry belongs in For you & your family; an invitation based on
  a particular observed interest belongs with that invitation's purpose.
- **Siblings and other adults:** Connection & feelings for the child's immediate
  interaction; For you & your family for family arrangements and adult cooperation.
  Do not duplicate advice merely to fill both topics.

## Age and scope are separate from topic

Retain 0-3, 3-6, 6-9, 9-12, 12-18, 18-24 months and 2-3 years as browsing bands.
Record exact source age wording separately from editorial placement. Where the
source gives a skill or interest instead of an age, keep that distinction. Baby
PDF p. 294 explicitly qualifies activity-list ages as guidelines; Toddler p. 20
(PDF p. 29) describes individual development.

An adult/family principle may be shared across all relevant bands without seven
copies. A source's toddler-wide or "all ages" statement must retain that context;
do not expand it automatically to newborns. A `3+ years` row does not authorize
inclusion as a 2-3-year invitation. Material beyond the third birthday is outside
scope unless it is needed as explicitly identified context for the covered period.
Pregnancy and birth preparation are excluded; newborn care and parent/family
experience after birth remain in scope, including mixed chapters.

Safety sensitivity is a cross-cutting evidence attribute, not a sixth topic.
Neither five topic labels nor three opening invitations justify weakly sourced
filler. Source findings determine depth; curating the final openings is later work.
