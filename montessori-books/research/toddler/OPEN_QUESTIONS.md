## Ages and table differences

The book's toddler scope is approximately 1-3 (PDF 14). The chapter-level activity
age caveat is on 33; the sensitive-period table's individual-timing caveat is on 26.
The activity chapter and appendix sometimes assign different numerical ages to
similar activities. Preserve each row's label, object/setup variant, interest and
readiness; do not average ages or silently use the earlier one. The activities
records contain the exact pairs and candidate-specific holds.

Particular tensions reported by the activity reader include scissors, sewing,

neighbor's numerical age. Cultural appropriateness qualifies appendix eye contact.

## Sensitive interpretations

- **Materials/access:** the general small-part/sharp-object supervision condition
  on 39 travels with each relevant activity. A natural-material preference, a
  lower price, a child-height shelf or a photographed tool does not establish
  independent suitability. Detailed tool access, sharpness, mouthing, water and
  climbing conditions remain in activity and care records.
- **Home:**76's classroom shelf example literally pairs 40 in with 120 cm; intended
  length is unknown; appendix 236 instead uses 47 in/120 cm and 16 in/40 cm height,
  whereas 76 uses 15 in/40 cm height. Both pages were independently checked. Do not
  invent a corrected specification.77 contains a sharp
  tool readiness/supervision note;78's accessible-bed suggestion includes room
  hazards but no complete sleep specification.79's bathroom and wash-product
  access examples are not independent bathing/product protocols.85 proposes
  high/bunk beds without toddler-age qualification; hold that concrete use.
- **Trust and injury:**95's no-worry/body-healing rhetoric cannot become a clinical
  assurance or permission to allow an injury.96 supplies protective duties and 189
  supports obtaining help for developmental differences; those qualifications do
  not independently validate the injury script.
- **Food/sleep/toileting:** the care records preserve author practices, anecdotes,
  specialist/doctor qualifications and unresolved practical applications. Snack
  restriction 77 and the later feeding discussion require explicit reconciliation
  as source descriptions; never combine them into a new hunger-management plan.
- **Emotional interpretation:**18's emphatic infographic is rhetoric, not proof of
  why an individual child cries or wakes. Observe rather than diagnose motives.
  Tentative translation 109 and the adult's own capacity remain part of the source.
- **Help/limits:**102's help-if-wanted paragraph describes elective activity;105
  and 110 retain protective intervention. The adult pause 194 has an immediate-danger
  exception. Neither concentration nor a calm-place idea justifies withdrawal of
  necessary help or punitive isolation.
- **Family safety:** the full separation discussion 210-211 carries explicit safety
  exceptions to contact. Mutual respect/shared values do not require contact or
  reconciliation in every family.113 and compressed appendix sibling-supervision
  examples do not establish that toddlers can supervise one another.
- **Adult distress:** physical self-care 188 includes seeking help when exhausted
  and a doctor's help for depression. Personal practices are presented as personal
  and not universally effective; neither calm rituals nor reflection substitute
  for the author's help-seeking language.

## Reuse candidates and meaningful differences

Use the foundation's eight stable principle keys as reuse candidates, each with
local evidence. Observation, readiness, participation with help, connection and
limits, time, home support, adult capacity/repair and family context can recur
without seven rewritten copies. Toddler-wide guidance alone does not establish a
newborn application; reconcile with the Baby research before broader placement.

The following are related but materially distinct:

- Specific feedback about an action and expressing warmth (112) versus assigning a
  fixed positive/negative identity (113).
- A prepared home that saves work (83-84) versus an aesthetic/minimalist requirement
  rejected by the modest-start and family-context framing (12,19,75,83).
- Flexible adult time examples (96,100-102,189-190) versus prescribed schedules.
- Reality-based imagination and pretend play (103) versus the author's fantasy
  claims. Do not rewrite this as a ban on all pretend play or an independently
  proven harm from fantasy.
- Child/family contributions versus compulsory independence, refusal of help or
  older-sibling caregiving duties.
- Practical participation versus prospective developmental benefit: source outcome
  claims stay attributed and never become guarantees in a title or illustration.
