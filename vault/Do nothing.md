The couple months after quitting my job, I was spending a ton of time wandering around NYC parks, processing the big changes of both leaving my job & that my wife was unexpectedly pregnant (more on those changes here: [[10.1.2024]])

For some reason I decided to sit down on a park bench, set a timer for 30 minutes on my watch, and did nothing at all. I wasn't aware this was a form of meditation until way later, but the description here matches exactly what I was doing: [youtube.com/watch?v=cZ6…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZ6cdIaUZCA)

"Let whatever happens, happen. As soon as you're aware of an intention to control your attention – drop that intention."

I kept doing this for 30 mins at a time in many different parks across NYC and across our subsequent moves to Northern Virginia & LA. One time I recall I decided to wake up and do absolutely nothing for 4 hours. I went outside after and had one of the most psychedelic walks of my life, comparable to taking shrooms.

## Nov 2026

Since late 2024 I've been doing nothing whenever it feels right for around 20m to an hour at a time. Ironically it's the best antidote I know to the feeling of not having enough time, that you're behind in your life, etc. Though this wasn't my intention with this practice, I've noticed that the voice in my head has become much quieter in the last two years. It's typically either silent or just objectively narrativizing what's going on, and is critical/mean only once in a while now.

Recently, after rereading [this article](https://sashachapin.substack.com/p/practice-nothing-the-most-profound) and completing [[The AoA Connection Course]], I realized that while my do nothing practice has been great for cultivating peace, stillness, and giving the voice in my head a well-deserved break, there is a lot of emotion that I've been subtly avoiding during my practice. Part of that I think is because I've always done do-nothing with my eyes open, so it ends up being more of a visual & head-based practice rather than body-based. The last few days in the mornings I've been doing nothing with my eyes closed and welcoming emotions and.. it's been intense!
