Got a tour of a local AMI-certified Montessori school (ages 2.5-6) and got to chat with the director. Posting my rough notes I brain dumped afterwards, organized loosely by topic

General:
- super inspiring to see a real Montessori classroom!! everything is kid-sized and it's insane to see in real life how peaceful & self-driven the work is
- there is A LOT OF PREP that goes into everything wow. physical space, its order, how every single thing is kid sized
- AMI teaches you philosophy, how to introduce materials, etc, but doesn't necessarily teach you how to "control the group" and handle the social dynamics. she said she has teachers just observe for like 6 months to a year before leading a classroom

Culture:
- kids are free to work on whatever they want, eat a snack, chat w others
- you will always "normalize" kids one at a time so even smaller class sizes are fine (pertinent for what i'm thinking for a small future microschool)
- they try to separate brothers/sisters across classrooms as much as possible to avoid those dynamics coloring school env + allow them to develop independently. parent <> teacher dynamics may be quite difficult too. important to keep in mind re: micrschool
- when new kids are being introduced, only one is added to the classroom at at time, so they have clear role models of how the classroom functions etc. clear culture stays intact. kids at the beginning are taught this is how we walk, this is how we sit in the class, etc.
- teachers are always sitting in a chair – kids come to them to ask for help, the only time they get up to go to kids is if the kid needs help there or introducing a new lesson. sitting = no policing
- seeing all this reminded me A LOT of [this blog post](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/07/culture-studies)

Lessons/materials:
- extremely specific progression of developmental activities
- lessons are always introduced 1:1. new lessons not introduced super frequently (wait for mastery). teachers introduce lessons when kids will have ~95% success rate at it, so they won't need much adult dependence. signs kid is done with a material: teach another kid how to use it, don't have any interest in it, etc
- lessons are introduced as turns - teacher shows how to do it, then leaves and kid does it themselves (so it doesn’t feel like policing/watching to make sure they do it right)
- if a kid tried their best and still left a mess that's fine, teachers make sure to clean it up later when they're not looking (so it's not "i tried my best and it wasn't enough for you")
- kids naturally find ways to rest when they need it – listen to music, sit at a table, go outside
